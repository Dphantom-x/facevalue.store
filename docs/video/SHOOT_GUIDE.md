# FaceValue Promo — Complete Shoot & Edit Guide

**For:** a first-time filmmaker • **Goal:** a ~42-second square (1:1) promo for X
**Camera:** Nikon Zf + external HDMI monitor • **Edit:** CapCut (desktop)
**Subjects:** a phone running the app demo on screen + some simple live-action

Read this once before you shoot. You don't need to memorize anything — every setting and menu name is written out below. Take your time; you can re-shoot as many takes as you want.

---

## 1. THE PLAN (read this first)

The Nikon Zf **cannot record a square video** — that's fine and totally normal. So here's the whole trick: you'll **shoot in regular wide 4K (16:9)**, use your monitor's **1:1 guide box** to see exactly where the square will be while you film, and then **crop it to a perfect square later in CapCut**. Shooting in 4K is the secret sauce — it's so high-resolution that after you crop to square you can *still* zoom in and push around the frame in editing without the video ever looking blurry. So: frame the square on the monitor, leave a little breathing room, record wide 4K, crop and punch in later. That's it.

---

## 2. GEAR CHECKLIST

**You must have these:**

- **Camera:** Nikon Zf.
- **Lens:** any Z-mount lens you own works. A 24–70mm zoom or a 35mm/50mm prime is ideal. (Settings below assume you can set aperture to f/4–f/5.6.)
- **External monitor:** a small on-camera HDMI monitor with a **1:1 frame guide** and **focus peaking**.
  - **Budget pick (~$120): FeelWorld FW568 V2.** Has focus peaking, histogram, and a "Safe Area" tool you'll use as your square guide. Modest brightness (~450 nits) — great indoors/shade, add a sunhood for bright sun.
  - **Sweet spot (~$160–190): FeelWorld F6 PLUS.** Brighter (~1600 nits, works outdoors), touchscreen, easy menus, pinch-to-zoom.
  - *(If you already own any HDMI field monitor with aspect/frame guides, just use it — you don't need to buy one.)*
- **The correct cable — this is specific:** a **micro-HDMI (Type D) → standard HDMI (Type A)** cable, **"High Speed" rated**, and **SHORT (~1–1.5 ft / 30–45 cm)**. The Zf's HDMI port is the tiny "micro" size, and long thin cables are the #1 cause of the picture dropping out. Buy short.
- **Power for the camera (so it doesn't die mid-take):** a **USB-C PD power bank or wall charger (30W or more)** + a **USB-C-to-USB-C cable**. The Zf can run off USB-C power continuously (setup in §3). A 45W bank can run it basically forever.
- **Tripod** + a way to hold the monitor: a **cold-shoe ball-head monitor mount** that slides into the Zf's **hot shoe** (the metal bracket on top of the camera). The monitor screws onto the ball head so you can tilt it to eye level.
- **The phone** you're demoing the app on.

**Nice-to-haves (not required):**

- A **sunhood** for the monitor (essential if you bought the budget FW568 V2 and shoot outdoors).
- A small **LED light** or a window for soft lighting (see §6).
- A **microfiber cloth** to wipe the phone screen (huge for killing smudges on camera).
- A **second tripod or a small stand/clamp** to hold the phone steady while you film its screen.
- Spare **EN-EL15c battery** as backup.

## 3. CONNECT IT (camera → monitor)

Do these in order. **Both devices OFF to start.**

1. **Mount the monitor** onto the camera's hot shoe (the metal bracket on top), using the ball-head mount.
2. **Plug the cable in:** small (micro/Type-D) end into the **camera's HDMI port**; big (standard/Type-A) end into the monitor's **"HDMI IN"** socket. Push gently and fully — the camera port is fragile. Leave a little slack so a tug can't yank the port.
3. **Plug in camera power:** USB-C cable from your power bank/wall charger into the camera's **USB-C** port.
4. **Power on the monitor FIRST, then the camera.**

**Now make the monitor picture "clean" (no icons on it).** On the camera, press **MENU**, go to the **SETUP menu (the wrench icon) → HDMI**, and set:

| Menu item (exact name) | Set it to |
|---|---|
| **Output resolution** | **Auto** |
| **Output range** | **Auto** |
| **Output shooting info** | **OFF**  ← this is the one that removes all the icons/clutter from the monitor |
| **Mirror camera info display** | **ON** (the camera does this automatically when the line above is OFF — it keeps your info on the *camera's* own screen) |

> Note: there is **no menu item literally called "Clean HDMI"** on the Zf. The toggle that gives you a clean image is **"Output shooting info → OFF."** Ignore any guide that says "turn on Clean HDMI."

**Turn on USB power** so the camera runs off the bank: still in the **SETUP menu**, find **USB power delivery → ON**. (The camera must be on and awake to actually draw power.)

**Check:** the monitor should now show a bare, clean image with no symbols on it. If the picture is black or missing, reseat the HDMI cable and confirm it's in "HDMI IN," not "HDMI OUT."

## 4. CAMERA SETTINGS (exact)

Press **MENU → VIDEO RECORDING menu** and set these:

- **Video file type:** **H.265 10-bit (MOV)** — and choose the **SDR** option (NOT "N-Log"). SDR looks normal and ready-to-use straight off the card; N-Log looks washed-out and needs special color work you don't want to deal with as a beginner.
- **Frame size / frame rate:** **3840 × 2160; 30p** (this is "4K UHD 30p").
- **Image area:** **FX** (full-frame — the best-looking 4K this camera makes).
  - ⚠️ Do **not** pick a 60p 4K option. At 4K/60p the camera forces a tighter "DX" crop and lower quality. Stay at **30p** so you can keep **FX**.

Now set the **exposure**. Turn the top dial to **M (Manual)** so nothing drifts between takes:

- **Shutter speed:** **1/50** (1/60 is also fine). This gives natural-looking motion. *Keep this fixed — it also helps avoid flicker when filming the phone screen; see §6.*
- **Aperture:** **f/4 to f/5.6.** Sharp, forgiving, and enough is in focus that focusing is easy. (Avoid shooting wide-open like f/1.8 for now — focus gets fussy.)
- **ISO:** keep it **as low as the light allows.** Base is **ISO 100**; **ISO 800** is a fine indoor starting point.
  - Easier option: turn **Auto ISO ON** (in M mode) and set **Maximum sensitivity ≈ 12800**. Then you only manage shutter + aperture, and the camera handles brightness without getting too grainy.
- **White balance:** **Auto2** ("keep warm colors") — easy and reliable. *(Optional, for the most consistent look: set a fixed number instead — about **5600K** for daylight, **3200K** for indoor warm bulbs — so the color can't shift mid-shot.)*
- **Autofocus mode:** use **AF-C** (continuous autofocus) with a **wide-area / subject-tracking** AF-area mode so it keeps your subject sharp. For the phone-screen shot, you can also tap the screen / use the focus point to lock onto the phone, then confirm sharpness with the monitor's zoom (see §5).

## 5. MONITOR SETTINGS

Open the **monitor's own menu** (buttons or touchscreen on the monitor itself — not the camera). Names vary slightly by brand; look for these:

1. **Turn on the 1:1 frame guide.** Find **Frame Guide** (may be called **Markers**, **Aspect**, **Guides**, or **Safe Area** on the FW568 V2).
   - Pick the **1:1** preset if it's listed.
   - If there's no 1:1 preset, choose **Custom aspect ratio** and enter **1.0** (or 1:1).
   - Optional: turn on the **mask/shade** option so everything outside the square gets darkened — this makes the square pop and is easier to compose in.
   - You'll now see a **square box** drawn inside your wide image. That box is your future crop.
2. **Turn on focus peaking.** Look for **Peaking** or **Focus Assist** and turn it on (red or yellow is easiest to see). Now a colored shimmer outlines whatever is **in sharp focus** — when your subject (or the phone screen) is outlined, it's sharp.
3. **Set brightness:** set the monitor's screen brightness to roughly the middle/high so you can see clearly, but don't judge your *exposure* by how bright the monitor looks — trust the histogram/peaking instead.

**The question you're wondering about — answered clearly:**

> **Does zooming/magnifying on the monitor change my recording? → NO.**

The monitor's **Zoom / Magnify / Punch-in** button just enlarges the picture **on the monitor's own screen** so you can check that something is perfectly in focus. It's like holding a magnifying glass up to the screen and taking it away again. **The camera keeps recording the full wide 4K frame** — the zoom changes nothing in your file. Use it freely: tap zoom, confirm the phone screen / your subject's eyes are crisp, zoom back out, record.

**How to use the 1:1 guide to "preview your crop":** Whatever sits **inside the square box** is what will survive after you crop in CapCut. So compose with that square in mind. **But frame a touch wide on purpose** — keep your subject comfortably inside the square with a little empty space around it. That extra room is what lets you **zoom in and reposition in editing** (the "punch-ins") without running out of picture. Think: *fill the square, but don't jam the subject against the edges of it.*

## 6. COMPOSE & SHOOT

**Framing:**

- Put your subject **inside the 1:1 guide box**, centered, with a little **headroom** (space above the head) and a little margin all around. Remember §5: leave breathing room so you can punch in later.
- For a tripod, locked-off shot, set the height to roughly **eye level** of your subject.

**Lighting basics (keep it simple):**

- **Soft light wins.** Face a **window** or put a soft light **in front of** the subject (slightly to one side), not behind them. Avoid a bright window *behind* the subject — that makes them a dark silhouette.
- Watch the monitor: if faces look too dark, add light or raise ISO a little; if highlights are blowing out (pure white blobs), reduce light or close the aperture slightly.

**Do several takes:** record **3–5 versions** of each shot. It's free, and having choices makes editing easy. Let each take run a couple seconds longer than you think — extra handles help in CapCut.

### How to film the PHONE SCREEN cleanly (the tricky part)

A phone screen is glossy and full of tiny pixels, so it fights back. Do this:

1. **Wipe the screen** with a microfiber cloth right before shooting — fingerprints are very visible on camera.
2. **Kill glare and reflections.** Tilt the phone (or the camera) a few degrees until you **don't see windows, lights, or yourself reflected** in the glass. Move overhead lights or the phone, not your patience — even a small tilt usually clears it. Shooting slightly off to the side rather than dead-flat helps.
3. **Crank the phone brightness to MAX**, and turn **off auto-brightness** so it can't dim mid-take.
4. **Turn on Do-Not-Disturb** (and airplane mode if you don't need data) so no notifications pop up on screen during a take.
5. **Avoid the screen "banding"/flicker and the shimmery moiré pattern:** keep the **shutter at 1/50** (from §4). If you still see scrolling dark bands or a rainbow shimmer on the screen, **nudge the camera distance/angle slightly** and try shutter **1/60** — one of those almost always clears it. Getting the camera square-on and steady also reduces moiré.
6. **Hold the phone rock-steady** — prop it on a stand/clamp or lay it flat and shoot from above. A still phone screen reads as crisp; a wobbling one looks soft.
7. Use the monitor's **zoom** to confirm the on-screen text is **sharp and readable** before you commit to the take.

## 7. POST IN CAPCUT (desktop)

You'll import the wide 4K footage, make the project square, crop the clip to fill the square, add your zoom/reposition moves, then drop your title and end cards on top.

### A. Start a square project and import

1. Open CapCut → **New project**.
2. **Import** your 4K clips (drag them in, or use the Import button) and **drag a clip onto the timeline**.
3. **Set the project to square:** in the **Player** (the preview window, top-center), open the **Ratio** dropdown and choose **1:1**.
   - *(Equivalent: click **Canvas** on the right-hand panel and choose the **1:1** preset.)*
   - In newer CapCut versions there may be no box to type exact pixels — that's fine. Just pick the **1:1** preset; you'll get exact 1080×1080 by choosing **1080p** later in Export.

### B. Crop/scale the clip to FILL the square

When you switch to 1:1, your wide clip will show **empty bars** on the sides. Fill the square:

1. **Click the clip** on the timeline. On the right, go to the **Video** tab → **Basic** section (this is where **Scale**, **Position**, **Rotate** live).
2. **Increase Scale** until the clip's top and bottom fill the square completely. The left and right edges will spill outside the frame — **that's correct** for a square crop.
3. **Drag the clip inside the Player** (or change **Position X/Y** in Basic) to slide it so your subject sits nicely centered in the square.

> Use **Scale** (not the separate Crop tool) for the square fill, because Scale can be **animated** — which you need for the zoom in the next step.

### C. Punch-in / zoom using Scale keyframes

This makes a smooth, slow zoom (e.g., a gentle push from 100% to 115%):

1. Select the clip → **Video → Basic → Scale.** Note the small **diamond (keyframe) icon** next to Scale.
2. Move the **playhead** (the vertical line on the timeline) to where you want the zoom to **start**. Click the **diamond** next to Scale — it sets a keyframe at the current value (your fill value, e.g. 130%).
3. Move the playhead to where the zoom should **end** (1–2 seconds later for a slow, classy push).
4. **Change the Scale value** higher (e.g., from 130% to ~150%). CapCut automatically makes the second keyframe and creates a smooth zoom between the two points.
   - *Snappy zoom instead of slow?* Put the two keyframes only a few frames apart.
   - *Smoother feel?* Right-click the clip → **Show Keyframe Animation**, then right-click a keyframe diamond → **Ease In / Ease Out** (or **Graphs**) so it eases in and out instead of moving robotically.

> **Quality note:** because you shot 4K, you can zoom in a lot and it still looks sharp. Just keep punch-ins **under ~180–200% Scale** to be safe and it stays crisp.

### D. Reposition / follow the action using Position keyframes

If your subject (or a finger on the phone) moves, pan to follow:

1. (Punch in a little with **Scale** first so you have room to move around without showing edges.)
2. **Video → Basic → Position** (the **X / Y** values, with their own **diamond** icon).
3. Playhead at the **start** → click the **diamond** next to Position (or drag the clip in the Player to frame the subject — that also sets it).
4. Move the playhead to where the subject has **moved to** on screen.
5. **Drag the clip in the Player** so the subject is re-centered. CapCut adds the keyframe and smoothly pans to follow.
6. Repeat at each point where the motion changes. Add **Ease In/Out** here too so the pan feels natural.

### E. Add the title-card and end-card overlays (with fades)

1. **Title card (front):** use **Text** (add a text layer) or import your title-card graphic onto a **track above** the video at the start. Set how long it shows by dragging its ends on the timeline.
2. **End card (last):** do the same at the end of the timeline.
3. **Add fades** so they appear/disappear gently:
   - For a **text** layer: in its settings look for **Animations → In / Out** and pick a **Fade** in and a **Fade** out.
   - For an **image/graphic** layer: select it → **Basic → Opacity**, and keyframe Opacity **0 → 100** over a few frames to fade in, then **100 → 0** to fade out (same diamond-keyframe method as Scale).

### F. Export

1. Click **Export** (top-right).
2. Set **Resolution = 1080p** (gives you the exact **1080×1080** square), **Frame rate = 30**, format **MP4**.
3. Export, then it's ready to post on X.

## 8. FIRST-TIMER TIPS + COMMON MISTAKES

**Do these:**

- **Lock everything in Manual** (M mode, fixed shutter/aperture/ISO or capped Auto ISO) so brightness doesn't flicker between or during takes.
- **Frame a little loose.** When in doubt, leave more room inside the square — you can always zoom in later; you can't add picture you didn't film.
- **Shoot extra takes and let them run long.** Choices in editing = a better final cut.
- **Check focus on the monitor** by zooming in before each take. The monitor zoom is free and changes nothing in your recording.
- **Wipe the phone, max its brightness, turn on Do-Not-Disturb** every single time before a phone-screen take.

**Avoid these common mistakes:**

- ❌ **Trying to make the camera shoot square.** It can't. Shoot wide 4K and crop in CapCut — that's the plan.
- ❌ **Picking a 4K/60p mode.** It secretly crops in and lowers quality. Stay at **4K 30p / FX**.
- ❌ **Shooting N-Log because it "sounds pro."** It looks flat and gray and needs extra work. Use **SDR**.
- ❌ **Using a long, cheap HDMI cable.** It causes the monitor picture to cut out. Use a **short** micro-HDMI cable, seated firmly.
- ❌ **Trusting the monitor's brightness to judge exposure.** Use the histogram / focus peaking instead; a bright screen can fool you.
- ❌ **Reflections in the phone screen.** Tilt the phone or camera a few degrees until the reflection of you/the window/lights disappears.
- ❌ **Letting the camera die.** Confirm **USB power delivery → ON** and that the bank is actually charging it before a long take.
- ❌ **Forgetting headroom for punch-ins.** If you fill the square edge-to-edge with your subject on set, you'll have nothing to zoom into in CapCut.
- ❌ **Filming with auto-brightness on the phone.** It dims mid-take and ruins the shot. Max brightness, auto-brightness off.

**If the monitor shows nothing:** reseat both ends of the HDMI cable, make sure it's in the monitor's **HDMI IN**, power the monitor before the camera, and confirm the camera is on and awake.

*That's everything. Frame the square on the monitor, leave breathing room, roll several 4K takes, then crop + punch in CapCut. You've got this.*
